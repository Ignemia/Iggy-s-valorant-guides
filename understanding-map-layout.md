# Understanding map layout
> Competitive tactical shooters have about 20 year long history. There were many attempts at map design but one has proven superior and now serves as the starting point for all maps we see in valorant. Them through making iterative changes the maps become unique but all of them start from the same conceptual layout.

## What does this layout look like? 
The layout in it's most barebones form has 7 main features placed pretty much symmetrically. 
1. Attack Spawn
2. Defender's Spawn 
3. Site A
4. Site B
5. Middle
6. Long
7. Short

Looking from above at this layout you would see Attacker's and Defender's spawns being placed opposite to one another. Sites A and B forming a cross line by which spawns are symmetrical. Spawns are connected through middle (this connection gets instantly closed off with first iteration of changes) and then Long and Short lead to each site one. Each site is connected to middle.

## What are Middle, Long and Short? 
These 3 staples of the original blueprint are there to serve a specific purpose.

### Short
This is entrance to a site which has short angles which can be held with a shotgun or SMG, it heavily favours attackers. 

### Long
This is entrance to a site which has one long open area to hold with a sniper rifle. Tjis part of the map heavily favours defenders. 

### Middle
This is a neutral zone usually broken down in later iterations by boxes and walls. It has a long angle sightline available for sniping but by being broken down by boxes and walls it creates opportunities to sneak up on the sniper or block their sightline completely by just smoking between boxes. This part needs to dtay heavily contested because usually control of middle is control of opponents rotation opportunities.
